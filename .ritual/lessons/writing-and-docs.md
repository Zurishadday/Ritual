# Writing and docs (this repository's lessons)

### P-5. "The X at the start and the close": every translator picks the same wrong reading

`origin: field · claude-opus-5-5 · 2026-10`

Merged into 54.

**The idea.** A qualifier in front of two items joined by "and" can attach to both or to the first only. The writer knows which one is meant; a reader has to guess, and translators guess alike.

**Example.** "What changed it is the package lessons at the start and the close" meant two pieces: the lessons given at the start, and the close step. All six translations read it as one thing, lessons given at the start and at the close. Two days later the same shape went into a new paragraph, and the translations misread it again.

**Detector question.** In this sentence, does any "the X at A and B" have two readings? Translated word by word, would the other reading come out?

**Warning sign.** A list of two where one item is a thing and the other is a moment or a place.

**What to do.** Make the split visible. Count it ("two pieces: …"), give each item its own article ("the lessons at the start, and the close"), or put the short item first ("the close, and the lessons at the start"). Then read the translation of that sentence for the reading you meant.

### P-6. A name you don't recognize is searched as written

`origin: field · claude-opus-5-5 · 2026-10` · `refines: 38`

Merged into 38.

**The idea.** When someone names a tool, a paper or a product I don't know, the closest name I do know is a guess. An answer about it sounds informed and is about something else.

**Example.** The person asked about "JEP", a cheap classifier they had seen online. It was taken for a well-known research architecture with a similar name, which was searched, explained and judged unfit at length. They meant a new product with that name; searching it as they wrote it would have found it at once.

**Detector question.** Is this the name the person wrote, or one I know that sounds like it? Did I search it as written before answering?

**Warning sign.** "They must mean…" · fixing the spelling of a name before searching for it.

**What to do.** Search the name exactly as written first, with the words the person used around it. If nothing matches, ask which one they mean, and offer the closest one I know as a question, not as the answer.
